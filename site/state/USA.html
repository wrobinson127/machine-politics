Wants new rules, not a treaty (REG-SOFT), since 2023-02-16, stated directly (EXPLICIT)

Supports new international rules that are not legally binding.

The United States launched the Political Declaration on Responsible Military Use of AI and Autonomy, a non-binding pledge, as its preferred new international measure, built on the existing laws of war.

The US-led non-binding declaration anchors military AI use in existing international humanitarian law.

use of AI in armed conflict must be in accord with States' obligations under international humanitarian law, including its fundamental principles

Political Declaration on Responsible Military Use of Artificial Intelligence and Autonomy, 2023-02-16 source

In its November 2023 explanation of vote, the United States says it is not the time to negotiate a legally binding instrument.

the United States believes that the time is not right to begin negotiating a legally binding instrument on LAWS

General Assembly First Committee, 78th session, 28th meeting, official verbatim record (A/C.1/78/PV.28), 2023-11-01 source

Keep it in Geneva (CCW-ONLY), since 2025-11-04, stated directly (EXPLICIT)

In November 2025 the United States voted against the UN resolution on autonomous weapons and explained why. It wants the work to stay inside the Geneva weapons convention, where decisions need consensus, and it objected to two specific paragraphs of the resolution. It did not say it opposes a future treaty. Its statement describes the Geneva track as working "on a future instrument and other measures". This replaces an earlier reading that had the US opposing any new rules; see the corrections page.

In its explanation of vote the US did not support and voted against the LAWS resolution.

the United States does not support L.41, the LAWS resolution, and voted against it

80th session of the UNGA First Committee, Cluster 4, Conventional Weapons: United States statement, 2025-11-04 source

The US framed its No as support for the CCW track working toward a future instrument, not opposition to a new instrument.

with a view to further deliberations and consensus among the CCW High Contracting Parties on a future instrument and other measures

80th session of the UNGA First Committee, Cluster 4, Conventional Weapons: United States statement, 2025-11-04 source

The US objection to the UNGA resolution was specific to two paragraphs, not to a new instrument as such.

the United States is deeply concerned about preambular paragraph 7 and operative paragraph 3

80th session of the UNGA First Committee, Cluster 4, Conventional Weapons: United States statement, 2025-11-04 source

The US stated strong support for the CCW GGE on lethal autonomous weapons systems.

this includes the ongoing work of the Group of Governmental Experts (GGE) on emerging technologies in the area of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems

80th session of the UNGA First Committee, Cluster 4, Conventional Weapons: United States statement, 2025-11-04 source

The US objects to the parallel UN General Assembly regulatory track for military AI and autonomy.

the creation of a new regulatory process under UN auspices would be inappropriate and undermine efforts to strengthen international peace and security

Resolution 79/239 Artificial Intelligence in the military domain: Submission by the United States of America, 2025-03-31 source

The US frames autonomy as a weapon-system capability governed by existing IHL classifications.

autonomy is a capability of the weapon system. This understanding means that LAWS fall under existing classifications in IHL

The Autonomy Aspect of Lethal Autonomous Weapon Systems (CCW/GGE.1/2025/WP.6), 2025-09-02 source

The US contemplates a future instrument or other measures within the CCW work.

any future instrument or other possible measures will need a common understanding of what systems are covered to be effective

The Autonomy Aspect of Lethal Autonomous Weapon Systems (CCW/GGE.1/2025/WP.6), 2025-09-02 source

The United States names the CCW as the forum for work on LAWS.

The CCW remains, in our view, the best and most credible forum for continuing to advance international efforts on laws.

DoD Directive 3000.09, Autonomy in Weapon Systems

Current version issued 25 January 2023; first issued November 2012. Quotes checked against official reproductions in the US submission to the UN Secretary-General and the ICRC casebook.

US doctrine requires design for appropriate levels of human judgment over the use of force.

Autonomous and semi-autonomous weapon systems will be designed to allow commanders and operators to exercise appropriate levels of human judgment over the use of force.
