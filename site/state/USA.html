REG-SOFT since 2023-02-16, EXPLICIT

Supports new non-binding measures.

The United States launched the non-binding Political Declaration on Responsible Military Use of Artificial Intelligence and Autonomy as its preferred new international measure, anchored in existing international humanitarian law.

The US-led non-binding declaration anchors military AI use in existing international humanitarian law.

use of AI in armed conflict must be in accord with States' obligations under international humanitarian law, including its fundamental principles

CCW-ONLY since 2025-11-04, EXPLICIT

Supports the CCW/GGE consensus process only, no stated outcome position.

In its First Committee explanation of vote (US Mission to International Organizations in Geneva, 4 November 2025) the United States explained its No vote on the LAWS resolution (L.41) as a defense of the CCW consensus track: it strongly supports the CCW and the Group of Governmental Experts, encourages a consensus outcome in 2026, and objects to the parallel UNGA resolution specifically at preambular paragraph 7 and operative paragraph 3. The stated core is venue preference, not opposition to a new instrument as such - the statement expressly frames the CCW track as working "on a future instrument and other measures." This supersedes the earlier provisional OPPOSE coding (see flags: correction).

In its explanation of vote the US did not support and voted against the LAWS resolution.

the United States does not support L.41, the LAWS resolution, and voted against it

80th session of the UNGA First Committee, Cluster 4, Conventional Weapons: United States statement, 2025-11-04 source

DoD Directive 3000.09, Autonomy in Weapon Systems

Current version issued 2023-01-25; the original issued November 2012. The canonical PDF blocks tool fetches; quotes verified via official reproductions in the US submission to A/79/88 and the ICRC casebook.

US doctrine requires design for appropriate levels of human judgment over the use of force.

Autonomous and semi-autonomous weapon systems will be designed to allow commanders and operators to exercise appropriate levels of human judgment over the use of force.
