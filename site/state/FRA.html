LBI-BAN since 2024-05-24, EXPLICIT

Supports a legally binding instrument that includes prohibitions.

France's submission for the Secretary-General's report states its preferred outcome plainly: a future instrument, possibly an Additional Protocol to the CCW, structured on a two-tier approach that prohibits fully autonomous systems and regulates partially autonomous ones. The prohibition is category-scoped (fully autonomous only), which this coding's scope note carries so France is not misread as supporting a blanket ban.

France states in its UN submission that it supports negotiating and adopting a new CCW Additional Protocol on LAWS.

France supports the negotiation and adoption of an Additional Protocol to the CCW

Avis sur l'intégration de l'autonomie dans les systèmes d'armes létaux (Defence Ethics Committee opinion on autonomy in lethal weapon systems)

Opinion of the Comité d'éthique de la défense (Ministère des Armées), dated 29 April 2021 on its face; the committee was seized by the Minister on 10 January 2020. Quotes below are from the Ministry's OFFICIAL ENGLISH version (translation: official). RULED CORE 2026-07-19: core = the opinion plus the government's adopting and citing statements (a 2025 parliamentary answer, QE 8402, cites it as the standing national reference framework). Formally an advisory opinion; in practice the government's standing framework. The opinion's own architecture is six guiding principles and 25 guidelines; it extends the refusal beyond use to manufacturing and export (P3), and traces the renunciation to the 5 April 2019 Saclay declaration (footnote 10). The URL carries a double .pdf extension in the ministry's own href; that is intentional, not a typo.

The French government has renounced the use of lethal autonomous weapon systems, and the Defence Ethics Committee endorsed that decision.

The French government has declared that it renounces the use of LAWS.
